# Handoff: validating experimental data for better AI scientists

**Main hypothesis:** training on experimental experience—actions, measurements, failures, context and revisions—improves an AI system's ability to investigate unfamiliar scientific problems. The ambition includes designing tests, using surprising results, revising explanations and making discoveries; experiment selection is one measurable component.

**Alternatives:** the data may mainly improve specialist prediction/optimization, with little benefit from recording richer scientific histories. It may instead support laboratory monitoring, robotics or a discovery business; those can be valuable, but need their own validation and customers.

## Purpose and comparison

Use existing datasets to establish **which kinds of experimental data improve which capabilities**, before commissioning new data. Public-data success establishes feasibility, not the unique value of our future dataset or a commercial moat. Code generation is routine implementation; the important choices are the learning comparison, held-out problems and trustworthy measurements.

Compare the same starting system unchanged, trained on outcomes, and trained on richer histories; include a strong conventional method. Match measurement access, tools and experimental budgets, and account for differences in training tokens/compute. Hold out whole studies, protein families, projects or laboratories—not related rows. Separate benefit from additional labels, metadata and chronological structure. Use feedback-shuffling/hiding controls where relevant; recorded choices are not automatically optimal choices.

**Measure capability, not narrative quality:**

- **Selection:** primary score is unique useful discoveries at a fixed measurement budget, using a threshold fixed before testing. Compare gains against both unchanged and outcomes-trained systems; also report discoveries over rounds and experiments saved.
- **Prediction:** use held-out error/ranking and calibration appropriate to the assay. Better prediction supports a prediction claim; selection must be tested separately.
- **Interpretation/recovery:** score against independently defensible experimental evidence or adjudication. Plausible explanations and disagreement between instruments are not ground truth.

Predeclare a practically meaningful gain. Report uncertainty across independent biological/problem groups; additional variants, cells, rounds and random seeds do not create independent studies. Replay may reveal only actually measured outcomes. Missing measurements and placeholder zeros are not failures; surrogate-generated outcomes must be reported as simulation.

## How the suggested /5 scores work

These are **ordinal priorities for validating the main hypothesis**, not experimental performance scores or probabilities of success. Judge each feasible experiment by directness to scientific capability, independent problem coverage, validity of its measurements/comparison, usable access and what decision its result would change. Coding difficulty has little weight.

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| **5** | Strong, actionable test of training-induced capability transfer on independent, preferably fresh/blinded experimental investigations. No listed experiment currently qualifies. |
| **4** | Broad, controlled retrospective test with meaningful independent holdouts; directly measures a scientific capability. |
| **3** | Worth running: a credible partial-transfer test or interactive evaluation, with important limits. |
| **2** | Narrow, adjacent or conditional evidence; useful for a specific capability/product or as supporting data. |
| **1** | Engineering check, representation resource or weak standalone validation of this thesis. |
| **Unscored** | Usable access or the actual evaluation protocol is not established; do not schedule a full study yet. |

Prediction-only and robotics-only tests are normally capped at **2 for this thesis**, even if attractive for a different business. Bundling small datasets earns no automatic upgrade: a score of 3 requires a defensible cross-campaign comparison. All scores below are proposed judgments; conditional access and dataset quality must be checked before execution.

Each #7 source is **2/5 standalone**; the conditional **3/5** applies to a defensible combined transfer test. Use each source deliberately: **ALDE** for variant selection and real-round forecasting; **SAMPLE** for uncertainty/failure handling; **metalloenzymes** for learning from exploration; **cell-free** for formulation transfer; **SDLGuide** for a small assay-history check. Original campaign records are not automatically complete records of scientific reasoning, and unmeasured alternatives remain unknown.

**Suggested handoff:** start with ProteinGym and HiTEA, reconstruct the usable biological campaign subset, and use measured-replay Autonomous Science Bench tasks as an evaluation adjunct. PRISM is the strongest lower-ranked addition to audit. Keep prediction and robotics alternatives explicit, and commission fresh experiments only after the validation identifies what data would matter.
